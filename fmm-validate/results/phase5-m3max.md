# Phase 5 scaling on the Apple M3 Max (T10, C5.3)

The distributed host FMM of Phase 5 on 1–12 ranks of the M3 Max (16 once): strong and
weak scaling stage by stage, the M2L strategy at p = 8, ranks against threads, overlap,
communication, redistribution, load balance, memory per rank and the errors against the
direct sum and the one-rank run (docs/phase5/T10-scaling.md, design
docs/design/distributed-fmm.md §11). Run on 2026-10-10, 11:17–12:56 (the sweeps of the
brief) and 12:57–13:17 (the strategy runs).

**Every figure below is one node over shared memory: ranks are processes on one Apple M3
Max, exchanging through Open MPI on that machine.** None is inter-node scaling. Every
time is *measured (M3 Max, ranks × threads as given, release build)*; the memory model
of distributed-fmm.md §9.2 is labelled *model*, and so is anything derived from it.

**Not measured:**
- **Inter-node scaling**: no cluster was available (docs/phase5/README.md, decision 2).
  The command that would measure it is in §12; no result is claimed from it.
- **The device on several ranks as a timing** (decision 3): §11 is a correctness table
  only.
- **The peak resident size**: macOS reports it only through `getrusage`, which needs a
  new dependency (libc); the "resident" column is `ps -o rss` at the end of the run.

- **The leaves scale**: their total over the ranks grows by at most 1.24× at 12 ranks.
- **At p = 3 the downward pass scales** (at most 1.11× at 12 ranks).
- **At p = 8 the downward pass stops scaling beyond 4 ranks in f64 and 8 in f32**: its
  total over the ranks grows 2.75× (cube) and 2.37× (Plummer) at 8 ranks in f64, 4.41×
  and 3.88× at 12; in f32 1.91× and 1.49× at 8 ranks, 3.49× and 2.73× at 12. The work
  per rank falls as 1/P (§8), so each M2L takes longer as ranks are added.
- The exchanges stay at most 24 ms (Plummer, f64, p = 8, 12 ranks: 0.5% of the wall
  time). The backward of the output grows to 146–478 ms at 12 ranks: it is the first
  exchange after the compute, so it holds the wait for the slowest rank as well as the
  return of 2.7–4 MB of output per rank (with the owners' input it is under 1 ms, §7).

The dense M2L at p = 8 applies one of 316 dense 81 × 81 tables per translation, target
by target; every rank holds its own copy, 16.6 MB in f64 and 8.3 MB in f32 (0.65 MB at
p = 3; sizes computed from the table shapes, not measured). The pattern above (no loss at
p = 3, a loss at p = 8 that sets in at fewer ranks in f64 than in f32, in the downward
pass only) is what per-rank copies of tables that compete for the shared caches and the
memory bandwidth would produce. Two measurements below test it: the strategies with
small tables (Rotation: O(p³) per translation from small rotation tables; Classes: 16
class matrices, 0.84 MB in f64), and ranks against threads, where threads share one copy.
This report has no cache counters, so the cause is inferred, not measured directly.

- **The small tables remove the limit.** Rotation keeps an efficiency of 0.94 (cube)
  and 0.90 (Plummer) at 8 ranks and 0.78 and 0.75 at 12; at 12 ranks it takes 688 ms
  against Dense's 2,752 ms on the cube (4.0×) and 1,616 against 4,626 ms on the Plummer
  sphere (2.9×).
- **Classes is the decisive case**: it performs Dense's products plus two O(p³)
  transforms per translation, so it is slower on one rank (10,104 against 8,169 ms on
  the cube, 1.24×; 1.18× on the Plummer sphere), but from 16 class matrices (0.84 MB)
  instead of 316 tables. It keeps 0.85 and 0.81 at 8 ranks and 0.69–0.70 at 12, and is
  faster than Dense from 8 ranks on (cube 1,494 against 2,580 ms at 8, 1,195 against
  2,752 ms at 12). More arithmetic from smaller tables scales; the same products from
  the large tables do not. **The per-rank copies of the dense tables limit strong
  scaling at p = 8**, not the communication, the balance or the arithmetic.

  time on the cube, 0.85× on the Plummer sphere), although `Auto` picks Dense up to
  p = 8. The leaves take the same time under every strategy (they do not depend on it).
- Nothing in the library was changed for this (the brief's rule); the consequences are
  in laplace-fmm-plan.md §7, Phase 5, "Recommendation for Phase 6".
