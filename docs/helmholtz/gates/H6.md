## Summary

- **The gate condition is met.** It asks for "the exponential
  M2L adopted where it wins, or kept opt-in (D8)". D8 was signed off on 2026-11-08 (CH6.2h)
  and amended twice on 2026-11-11 (CH6.1g, CH6.1h). `HelmholtzM2l::Auto`, the builder's
  default, now takes the exponential M2L on every level of degree p_l ≥ d_pw whose κ_l lies in
  FMM3D's tables with Im κ_l < 5π, at every ε ≥ 10⁻⁸, in f64. Below 10⁻⁶ it raises the M2L's
  degree to max(p_l, 28) or max(p_l, 32).
- **What it gains.** At 10⁻⁶ the M2L takes 0.03–0.70× the previous `Auto`'s time on the three
  machines, and the evaluation 0.05–0.91× (CH6.2h; on locust two ms-scale cases at
  1.05–1.10×, within their spread). At 10⁻⁷ and 10⁻⁸ the evaluation takes 0.43–0.83× and
  0.31–0.71× Rotation's on every case above 0.1 s (CH6.1h, Kathleen), and 0.27–0.65× on the
  two large cases (§8). At 10⁻³ it changes
  little: the M2L 0.47–1.19×, mostly the same methods, since p_l is below d_pw there. The errors stay within
  1.1× Rotation's everywhere measured.
- **All 17 tasks are done**, CH6.1a–h and CH6.2a–i (#179–#204), each merged with its record or
  report and a progress entry. No task has a pending run, and all 31 questions they raised
  are signed off.
- **CI on `main` is green.** Every H6 merge passed every job. One run in the period (#188,
  CH7.5b, an H7 task) was cancelled by run-tests' 30-minute limit, during a cold-cache build
  after a toolchain release, not a test failure (§1.3).
- **One gap, closed on this branch.** The default's widest change, the exponential M2L at
  10⁻⁷ and 10⁻⁸, had been verified on CH6.1e's 26 cases of at most 1.1·10⁵ points only. It
  had never run on D1's two large cases, W1 at kD = 500 and W3 at kD = 100: CH6.1e's sign-off

## 3. Measured against the proposal

### 3.1 The case for the exponential M2L (§5.3, §7)

§5.3 gave three reasons to expect the exponential M2L to matter:
- **Laplace's gain.** Phase 6 T10 measured the plane-wave M2L at 2.2–9.8× the faster of
  Batched and Rotation at f64 p ≥ 8.
- **The degrees.** p = 7–24 in D1's range, where an O(p²) M2L gains.
- **The work.** The low-frequency levels carry most of the far field below kD ≈ 10³.

It also quoted Gumerov and Duraiswami: RCR about as efficient as exponential forms at
moderate p.

Measured:

| ε | p_l (plateau, CH3.3) | the exponential M2L over Rotation's M2L | the evaluation over the previous `Auto`'s / Rotation's |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10⁻³ | 7 | rarely chosen (p_l < d_pw); on levels 2–3 up to 2.2× slower | the M2L 0.47–1.19× `Auto`'s (the same methods on most cases) |
| 10⁻⁶ | 15 | 0.19–0.61× (M6, locust, Kathleen; CH6.2b, CH6.2c) | 0.05–0.91× `Auto` (CH6.2h; two ms cases on locust to 1.10×, within spread) |
| 10⁻⁷ | 20, raised to 28 on the M2L | 0.37–1.32× (CH6.1h) | 0.43–0.83× Rotation above 0.1 s |
| 10⁻⁸ | 24, raised to 32 | 0.24–0.89× (CH6.1h) | 0.31–0.71× Rotation above 0.1 s |

- **Both readings hold, in different ranges.**
  - At 10⁻³ (p ≈ 7) Gumerov and Duraiswami's reading holds: RCR and the waves are at par,
    and Batched is often faster.
  - From 10⁻⁶ the exponential M2L wins 1.6–5× on the M2L.
  - The gain is smaller than Laplace's T10 at the same nominal p. The reason: below 10⁻⁶
    the waves need a degree above the level's, and above 10⁻⁶ they run at p_l ≈ 15, where
    Rotation is still cheap.
- **Where the time goes.**
  - Merge and shift is 64–95 % of the exponential M2L per level (CH6.2d §3).
  - On levels 2–3 it was held to one and eight threads by the drivers' parent grain, until
    CH6.2f's split by child.
  - The conversions of the waves to locals are 76–90 % of those levels' thread time
    (CH6.2f).
- **The far-field split at kD = 500** (§7's "the exponential M2L would cut it further"):
  - On Kathleen at 10⁻⁶ the exponential M2L takes 0.50× Rotation's M2L on W1 at kD = 500
    and 0.24× on W3 at kD = 100 (CH6.2c).
  - One call of W1 at kD = 500 now takes 22.4 s against FMM3D's 61.6 and 43.2 s (CH6.2d).
    Our RCR on the high-frequency levels and FMM3D's per-call setup weigh in.
  - At kD = 500 the remaining far-field time is mostly the high-frequency levels' RCR, which
    supports H4 next (§5).

### 3.2 The quadratures (§5.3, R7, S4)

- §5.3: "a level needs its own quadrature (generated or tabulated; spike S4)". By CH0.4's
  sign-off it is tabulated: FMM3D's tables for iprec 1–3, 437 domains each, 1.68 MB with
  NIST's notice.
- The α counts are ours, by a Bessel bound to the region's corner ρ = 4√2. That is 1.31–1.51×
  FMM3D's waves where Im κ < π/2.
- CH6.2e and CH6.2g measured whether fewer would do:
  - the FMM held at reach 4.5;
  - the worst pair did not, up to 2·10⁴× the stated error near Re κ = 16π;
  - so the corner stays.
  - FMM3D's own counts give it 30–100× our error on one plane-wave level at iprec 1
    (CH6.2d §4). That fits its misses of ε at 10⁻³ on three cases (§3.5).
- The precision does not follow `hwts3e`'s ε alone.
  - At the FMM's degrees, iprec 3 at ε = 10⁻⁸ errs 14–25× RCR's on the pair chain (CH6.1c),
    because a degree-p_l multipole has a truncated tail the quadrature does not resolve.
  - The fix is the resolving degree p_w (CH6.1f, CH6.1g). FMM3D, whose degrees are generous,
    does not meet this problem in the same form.
- **The build cost** (§7, "the quadratures of the exponential M2L per level"; R7's
  "precomputation complexity"): the tables are parsed once. A build at p_w takes 2.1–9×
  Rotation's on CH6.1h's cases, all under 1 s, the RCR tables at p_w included. R7's first
  half is retired.

### 3.3 The degrees (§3.5)

§3.5's calibrated rule (CH3.3) gives each level one degree p_l for every operator. Since
CH6.1g a level with waves below 10⁻⁶ has two:

| ε | p_l (plateau) | p_w, the M2L's | the form | operators at p_w |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| 10⁻⁷ ≤ ε < 10⁻⁶ | 20 | max(p_l, 28) | `M2l` | the M2L, the level's stores, M2M from and L2L to the children |
| 10⁻⁸ ≤ ε < 10⁻⁷ | 24 | max(p_l, 32) | `Leaves` | all but the M2M to the parent and the L2L from it |

- The FMM's error at 10⁻⁸ falls to 0.003–0.48× Rotation's (CH6.1g), so the raise buys
  accuracy as well as a working quadrature.
- §3.5 does not mention this second degree. Correction C2.

What worked:

- **Splitting before starting.** H6 began as two tasks and closed as 17. Each split was taken
  by `/next-task`'s size rule and recorded in the roadmap, and every PR stayed reviewable.
- **A check written before the change it guards.**
  - CH6.2g wrote the per-pair test first, to adopt reach 4.5, which CH6.2e had recommended
    and the user had signed off. The test failed at up to 2·10⁴×, the user withdrew the
    sign-off, and the default never changed.
  - CH6.1g did the same mid-task: iprec 2 at 10⁻⁷, recommended by CH6.1f, failed in the FMM
    and was asked again before anything merged.
- **D8 as a revisited decision.** It was signed off at 10⁻⁶ and amended twice as CH6.1g and
  CH6.1h measured more. Each amendment is in the decision table with the user's words.
- **Machines.** Kathleen's outage (CH6.2b) moved its runs to a task of their own (CH6.2c)
  without holding the list. The quiet gates on the M6 and locust kept timings clean, and the
  records name each job, node and quiet window.

What to change:

- **`/next-task`: a rule's wider scope widens its verification set.** CH6.1e's sign-off 1
  left the large cases out because Rotation ran there. When CH6.1g and CH6.1h put waves
  there, nothing brought the cases back (§2). When a task lifts a bound or widens a default,
  the plan should list the cases earlier sign-offs excluded for the old scope, and run them or
  say why not.
- **Pair evidence and FMM evidence, both, before a numerical rule changes.** Twice one of
  them misled:
  - the pair chain's worst case recommended iprec 2 at 10⁻⁷, and the FMM refuted it
    (CH6.1f → CH6.1g);
  - the FMM's sampled errors recommended reach 4.5, and the worst pair refuted it (CH6.2e →
    CH6.2g).

  A standing rule in `/next-task`, both measures before a sign-off on a quadrature, degree or
  count, would have saved one task each time.
- **Proposal amendments need their Appendix A line.** CH0.4, CH6.2h, CH6.1g and CH6.1h each
  amended the proposal in place with no line in Appendix A (C8). `/next-task`'s logging step
  should add the line with the amendment.
- **Debug test time.** `helmholtz_fmm`'s one-rank run at opt-level 0 grew to 715 s on a
  loaded M6 (§2). CI's opt-level 1 runs it in under two minutes. Two options: `/verify` runs
  the MPI crates' tests at `CARGO_PROFILE_DEV_OPT_LEVEL=1` with its own target directory, as
  CI does, or the next Helmholtz tests trim the debug scenarios.
- **The roadmap as a log.** Done lines carry their whole history (question 6). `/next-task`
  should keep a ticked line to its result and pointers, and `/milestone-gate` condense a
  closed milestone.
