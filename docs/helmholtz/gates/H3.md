### 3.1 Truncation (§3.5)

The formula of §3.5 against the calibrated rule. At kD = 500 and ε = 10⁻⁶ the "p by level"

| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 19.9 λ | 144 | 131 |
| 3 | 9.9 λ | 82 | 72 |
| 4 | 5.0 λ | 50 | 41 |
| 5 | 2.5 λ | 33 | 25 |
| 6 | 1.2 λ | 25 | 16 |
| 7 | 0.6 λ | 23 | 15 |

The top level against kD, formula and then the rule, at 10⁻⁶ and 10⁻⁸:

| kD | formula | rule |
| ---: | --- | --- |

- **The low-frequency plateau** (CH3.3) is 7, 9, 12, 15, 20 and 24 at 10⁻³ … 10⁻⁸. That is
  about half of S7's 16 … 46, and far below the formula's floor of 22.
- **The comparison with Laplace.** §3.5 compares with Laplace's degree 12 for 10⁻⁶ in φ.
  Helmholtz needs 15 at 10⁻⁶ with φ and ∇φ both within ε/2, so it is consistent with
  Laplace: the gradient sets the degree.
- **The high-frequency levels.** The excess p − x grows about linearly in L = log₁₀(1/ε),
  faster than the formula's (ln 1/ε)^{2/3}. That is CH4.3's to refit.

### 3.2 Strategies (§5.3)

- **The degree range.** §5.3 expects "Helmholtz low-frequency levels run at p of 20 to 60".
  Measured in D1's range: 7 to 24 on levels with x ≤ 4, and up to about 38 on intermediate
  levels at kD = 100. That is the range where table-based M2L can still pay.
- **Dense.** "At p = 20 the 316 offsets take about 1 GB per level": measured 983 MB
  (CH3.2a), confirmed. Dense is faster per pair only up to p = 5.
- **Classes.** "At the Helmholtz p of 20 to 60 its cost is measured, not assumed":
  - the transforms are about half a pair's time at p = 3 and 6–11 % from p = 12;
  - Classes beats Rotation per pair only up to p = 4;
  - its tables are 18–20× smaller than Dense's.
- **Batched.** "Laplace's Batched strategy carried over on the shared drivers": built as
  its own Helmholtz block, by the user's decision (CH3.2c), with the roadmap carrying the
  change. Fused, it beats Rotation per pair at p = 7 by 1.84× (M6), 1.77× (locust) and
  4.52× (Kathleen).
- **Auto** exists, by degree and machine class (CH3.2d). The exponential M2L (H6) is not
  built yet.

### 3.3 The cost model (§7) and D6

§7, W1 at kD = 500, ε = 10⁻⁶: Rotation's M2L work counted at 2P³ complex multiply-adds per
pair, P = p + 1, with §7's box counts and V lists. §7's own figures reproduce exactly at
this count: 95.0 × 10⁹ on level 7, and 62 × 10⁹ for levels 2–5 on RCR. The "calibrated"
column below is that same count with the measured degrees of §3.1. **It is an estimate,
not a measured time.**

| Levels | §7, formula p (10⁹) | calibrated p, estimate (10⁹) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 6–7 (low frequency, Rotation) | 125.8 | 36.7 |
| 2–5 (high frequency, all RCR as in H3) | 61.5 | 37.0 |
| low-frequency share | 67 % | 50 % |

- **The low-frequency levels.** The calibration cuts their work 3.4-fold. §7 predicted
  "several-fold" if the degree came near Laplace's.
- **The high-frequency levels.** Their degrees fall less, about 1.3–1.8-fold in work.
- **The high-frequency premise.** "The low-frequency levels carry most of the far-field
  work" at kD = 500 becomes "about half" once p is calibrated, before Auto speeds up the
  low-frequency levels further.
- **The low-frequency premise.** Most of D1's range lies at kD ≤ 100, where the levels are
  low frequency or intermediate. There, and at tight ε, our time against FMM3D is worst:
  - W4 cube at kD = 1 and 10⁻⁸: 1.66 s, against 0.86 s (FMM3D default) and 0.33 s
    (fastker);
  - W3 at kD = 100 and 10⁻⁸: 129 s against 38 s and 28 s;
  - W1 at kD = 100 on one thread at 10⁻⁸: 57.9 s against 23.1 s and 8.9 s.

  The diagonal form of H4 changes nothing on those trees. FMM3D's lead there comes from
  its plane-wave M2L, which is H6.

So **H6 first (D6, the proposal's default) stands**, on a sharper reason than §7's: H6
targets the cases where we are slowest, across most of D1's range. At kD = 500, H4 and H6
address shares of the far field of about the same size.

### 3.4 P2P (§5.7)

- **The phase range.** §3.7 and §5.7 expect phases k r up to about 22 c_leaf = 22. The
  contract holds to 2¹⁰.
- **The cost.** §5.7 expected the cost per pair to be "several times Laplace's". It is
  3.8–5.9×. After CH3.4c the P2P is a small share of the FMM's time: 2–9 % faster
  end to end at 10⁻³, under 1 % at 10⁻⁶. The translations dominate.

### 3.5 Practical record (§11)

- **The authors' 2009 code** solved kD = 500 with 1.5 million unknowns in 4.7 GB on a
  4-core PC.
- **H3, W1 at kD = 500** (2.0 million points), one Kathleen node (40 cores):
  - 32, 52 and 100 s at 10⁻³, 10⁻⁶ and 10⁻⁸, build included, all-RCR, Rotation M2L;
  - FMM3D took 49, 63 and 72 s (default) and 33, 44 and 49 s (fastker);
  - memory not measured at these degrees (§2).

## 5. Recommendations for the next milestones

- **The order: H6 before H4 (D6).** Every gate case at kD ≤ 100 is a low-frequency or
  intermediate tree, and the largest gaps to FMM3D are there (§3.3).
  - CH0.4 (S4, the plane-wave quadratures per k w_l) is H6's first need and is
    runnable now.
  - CH6.1 needs CH0.4, CH3.1 and D6.
- **Before H4's diagonal form:**
  - the worst-box concentration, on all-RCR trees where the cause is not a class
    boundary;
  - the degree rule at kD ≈ 1000, 10⁻⁸ on Kathleen, also on all-RCR.

  Both are cheaper to read before the diagonal form adds a second source of error.
  Question 4 proposes them as roadmap tasks.
- **The open gates.** HR, H0, H1 and H2 are still open:
  - HR waits for CHR.3;
  - H0 waits for CH0.4, CH0.5, CH0.6, CH0.8b and CH0.9;
  - H1 waits for CH1.3d;
  - H2 waits for CH2.1, CH2.2, CH2.3 and CH2.5.

  None of them blocks H6. H4 needs CH0.6 (S6) and so H0's spikes. With the priority list
  finished, `/next-task` now takes the first runnable task in file order, which is CHR.3
  (needs CH0.9), so effectively CH0.6 or CH0.8b. A new priority list for the path to H6
  (CH0.4, CH6.1, CH6.2) would keep the loop on the decision this gate recommends
  (question 6).
- **Memory.** CH4.1's scratch-budget task should also report the resident memory of the
  RCR FMM on W1 at kD = 500 at the calibrated degrees, as the baseline the signature
  scratch is added to.
- **The node budget.** Before any multi-rank run with Auto as the default, the Helmholtz
  dense budget should count per node (constraint 7), as Laplace's node rule does. This

What worked:

- **Splitting before starting.** Four of H3's tasks were split (CH3.0, CH3.1, CH3.2,
  CH3.4) and one added (CH3.2d). Sixteen task PRs merged in three days, each reviewable, with

- **The quiet gate and the rebuild rule.** Both caught a timing of a stale binary
  (CH3.2a's first runs) before it reached a report.

What to change:

- **`/next-task`: a follow-up needs a roadmap line.** When a sign-off creates a follow-up
  ("a follow-up before H4"), the same PR should add a roadmap line for it. CH3.2d did
  (CH4.0); CH3.6 and CH3.3 did not, and this gate had to find them in the progress log.

  tests to a CI job, compare the job's time on the PR with its `timeout-minutes` and flag
  anything above about two thirds. The x86_64 SIMD job crossed its limit unnoticed, because
  a cancellation on `main` after the merge is seen by no step of the loop.

  tests should trim it: fewer degrees, or release-only checks.

  (this gate does).
